# Stop Hunt, Fake Prices - Best Kept Swing Trading Strategy behind Price Manipulation

> Binni's tutorial on the Wash and Rinse (WR) pattern, explaining how it works as a stop hunt strategy and how to trade it in high-probability locations.

## Key claims

- **Stop Hunt Rationale**: Algos and professional traders can see obvious retail resistance and support levels (where stops are placed). They push prices to flush these stops, creating fake breakouts to induce fear or greed before reversing. (01:34, 02:41)
- **Wash Line as a Boundary**: The wash line acts as a strict divider between bullish and bearish zones. If price stays above it, it is a bullish zone; below it, bearish. (04:13)
- **Bullish Wash and Rinse Criteria**:
  1. Price must be heading lower initially. (06:23)
  2. There must be some form of support (historical levels, MAs, etc.). (06:54)
  3. The wash bar is usually a long, solid bar (representing impulse behavior to flush stops). (07:26)
  4. Entry is valid only when a subsequent rinse bar closes above the wash line. (08:03)
- **Bearish Wash and Rinse Criteria**: Similar in reverse, requiring continuous upward movement, closing higher than a previous high, hitting resistance, and a rinse bar closing below the wash line. (08:26)
- **High Profit Locations**: WR patterns are most reliable near important price levels (previous highs/lows, historical support/resistance) and near moving averages when trading with the trend. (10:09, 11:46)
- **Cross-Asset Applicability**: The pattern works on stocks, Forex, Gold, and Oil, often tricking traders into the wrong direction near critical historical levels. (12:41 - 20:41)
