# Retest Execution

> The core TAT trading discipline of waiting for price to retest a broken horizontal level or structural support before entering a trade, rather than buying initial breakout candles.

## Definition

The **Retest Execution** rule dictates that an initial breakout candle (impulse bar) should never be bought directly. Instead, traders must wait for price to return and retest the broken structural boundary (or test a support level a second time) on lower timeframes (H1/M15) to confirm institutional backing and optimize risk-to-reward parameters.

## Origin

Formulated by Bini Ong as a foundational technical trading rule within the TAT trading methodology to eliminate emotional chasing of green breakout bars and avoid momentum traps.

## Key Claims

1. **The 2nd Test Rule**: A single touch of a support/resistance level is unverified and untrusted. A boundary is only validated when price returns for a 2nd test and prints a lower-timeframe reversal signal.
2. **Breakout vs. Retest Priority**: *"It's not the break that's important; it's that retracement retested level that's important."* Initial breakout candles widen stop-loss boundaries unnecessarily.
3. **Multi-Timeframe Cascading Retest**:
   - **Daily / H4**: Identify the macro structural retest zone (e.g. 5-wave retracement into 2nd support test).
   - **H1**: Confirm directional impulse breaking horizontal TAT resistance clusters.
   - **M15**: Execute entry on the M15 pullback as price retraces back down to test the broken H1 resistance as new support.
4. **Risk-Scale Optimization (1-to-10 Scale)**: Entering on the lower-timeframe retest secures entries at level 1 or 2 on Bini's 1–10 risk scale (where 1 = support bottom, 10 = resistance top), yielding maximum risk-to-reward ratio.
